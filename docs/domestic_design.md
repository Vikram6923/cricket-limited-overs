# Domestic cricket: Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy and other domestic cups (research, 2026-11-10)

Status: steps 1-5 of the build plan are done: the Mushtaq Ali Trophy plays in the game (Seasons & careers ->
Mushtaq Ali Trophy). Left: State career (6) and the one-day cups (7); section 7 has the open questions.

The question: can the game add domestic tournaments, with the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy (SMAT) first, possibly under
a new menu? Short answer: **yes for SMAT, but ratings come first.** Cricsheet has 695 SMAT matches, but today the
game rates only 338 of the 1,489 players in them, and 36 of the 38 state teams in 2021-22 can't field 11 rated
players. Adding SMAT to the T20 rating data fixes that and also improves the IPL's uncapped players. The 50-over
Vijay Hazare Trophy is not on Cricsheet, so it can't be rated from data at all.

What this means:

- **Ratings.** 1,489 players have played SMAT on Cricsheet; 338 are rated today and 326 have played in the IPL.
  Of the unrated, 453 have faced or bowled 120+ SMAT balls and 186 have 300+. In 2021-22, 36 of 38 teams have fewer
  than 11 rated players (Karnataka 12/18 and Punjab 12/13 are the only exceptions; Sikkim, Tripura, Services,
  Arunachal and Manipur have none). The T20 rating model already handles leagues as their own tier with a team level
  per side, and the 326 IPL players link SMAT to the IPL, so `sma` can join the T20 data like the 11 leagues. Side
  benefit: the IPL's uncapped players, rated today on a few IPL games, get their domestic record too.
- **Real-season replays** (League Season style, real squads and groups) work for **2018-19 to 2021-22** (and
  roughly 2015-16). Recent seasons are too thin to rebuild real squads and groups from Cricsheet.
- **"This season" squads** (2025-26) would have to come from each player's latest state team in the data (620
  players from 2022-23 on, 33 of 38 teams), topped up from the published squads (Wikipedia / BCCI) matched by name to
  Cricsheet's register. Players with no data at all can't be rated, so the north-eastern Plate sides would be thin.
- **Conditions.** SMAT scores lower than the IPL: about 7.3-7.9 runs an over in 2016-2023 against the IPL's
  8.3-9.0 (9.6+ from 2024); sixes are rarer (about 5 an innings against 6-10). The engine's competition baseline
  would come from the data, as it does for each league.
- **One-day cups.** The ODI model has no league tier: it is fitted on internationals only (plus a List A prior from
  Wikipedia). The English and Australian one-day cups would need that tier built; the English cup is also weak as a
  showcase now, because its best players are at the Hundred at the same time (9-14% of appearances rated since 2021).

## 3. What the game can already do, and what's missing

| Need | Today | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| Squads by season | `scripts/build_league_presets.py` builds every franchise season | an `sma` (and `ctc`) entry; no overseas limit (0); real groups per season |
| Ratings | T20 = T20Is + 11 leagues | add `sma` (and `ctc`); the league list is hard-coded in about 12 places (fetch, raw stats, presets, 6 fits, `validate_leagues.py`, `web_ui.py`, `webui/app.js`, `webui/teams.js`): move it to one list first |
| Groups of unequal size | `play_tournament(groups=[...])` takes explicit lists | — |
| 4 points a win | `points_table(win_points=...)` exists | pass it through |
| Knockouts | `semis`, `ipl`, `final`, `none` | **quarter-finals** (A1 v B2 ...), **preliminary quarter-finals** (5 groups), **Super League** (two groups of 4, winners to the final) |
| Plate division, promotion and relegation | — | a second small league in the same run; promotion only matters in a career |
| Home grounds | each league side plays half its games at home | SMAT has none: use the season's real host venues |
| Impact Player | IPL from 2023 | also SMAT 2022-23 and 2023-24 |
| Team identity | colours and short codes for franchises and nations | 38 states: short codes (MUM, TN, KAR, J&K ...) and plain state colours |

## 4. Proposed menu: a fifth door, **Domestic cricket**

Home has four doors (Play a friend, Seasons & careers, Auction & draft, What-if sandbox), each opening 2-5 cards. A
fifth door keeps domestic cricket in one place instead of hiding it in League Season's list of leagues:

**Domestic cricket**
1. **Mushtaq Ali Trophy** — a real season (2018-19 to 2021-22, and "This season" with the latest squads), real
   groups and that season's format: groups then pre-quarters / quarters (older seasons) or the Super League
   (2025-26). Captain any state, ball by ball, as in League Season. The Plate runs alongside.
2. **State career** — run a state for years: Elite and Plate with promotion and relegation, players ageing and
   newcomers (career mode's machinery without the auction), and your best uncapped players showing up in the IPL
   auction. This is idea A3 in `docs/ideas.md` ("domestic cricket as a talent pool").
3. **Domestic T20 elsewhere** — T20 Blast, Super Smash and the CSA T20 Challenge (the first two stay in League
   Season too).
4. **One-day cups** (later, needs the ODI league tier) — England's One-Day Cup and Australia's One-Day Cup.
   Vijay Hazare only if its data ever reaches Cricsheet; it is not on the list until then.

The cost: a fifth door goes against "at most four choices" on the home screen. The alternative is a card inside
Seasons & careers ("Domestic cups") with the same contents one tap deeper.

## 5. Build plan

Each step that touches the engine ends with the usual calibration check (replay real matches with their real XIs)
and stops once its gate is met.

1. **One league list.** *Done 2026-11-10.* The 12 hard-coded lists now come from `engine/leagues.py` (code,
   short and long label, home country, season start month, UI badge); the browser gets labels and badges from
   `/api/meta` (`league_info`). Every derived list was checked equal to the old one.
2. **SMAT in the ratings.** *Done 2026-11-10 (SMAT only; `ctc` is one more line in `engine/leagues.py` and a
   rebuild).* `sma` is in the league list with `league_season=False` (rated and fitted, but not offered in League
   Season) and `side_level=True`: players whose main competition is SMAT are shrunk toward their **state's** level
   (`league:sma:Mumbai`, a "side" tier of its own), not one SMAT-wide level. 1,468 of the 1,489 SMAT players are now
   rated (T20 ratings: 6,457 -> 7,587 players) and every 2021-22 state can field 11 rated players. All T20 fits
   that read league data or ratings were refitted. Results and the gate are in `docs/engine_progress.md`
   ("Mushtaq Ali Trophy in the T20 ratings"): T20I and IPL replays are as good as before or better (one exception:
   IPL chases won 49.2% v 51.6% before, real 52.4%); the SMAT replay (435 matches, 2019-2021) gets averages,
   wickets and phase run rates within 2-3%, but the **spread is too narrow** (first-innings SD 32.9 v 40.0 real):
   weak Plate sides lose by less than they really do. That part of the gate is not met; see risks.
3. **Formats.** *Done 2026-11-10.* `play_tournament` (engine/tournament.py) has `knockout="qf"`, `"pre_qf"`,
   `"super_league"` (winners to the final, 2018-19 and 2025-26) and `"super_league_semis"` (2019-20); `advance`
   per group (a list); `plate={"teams", "rounds", "groups", "knockout", "advance"}` plays the Plate in the same run
   (its tables and final under `result["plate"]`, `promoted`, `relegated`; `advance` puts the Plate's top sides
   into the Elite knockouts as the lowest seeds, as in 2020-21); `match_count` counts all of it. Qualifiers from
   several groups are seeded by position, then points and NRR; quarter-final ties and Super League groups keep
   group-mates apart where possible. The existing brackets give identical results to before. Five new tests in
   `tests/test_tournament.py`. Not done: the real seasons' exact fixtures and seeding rules (step 4 reads them
   from the data where it can).
4. **Presets.** *Done 2026-11-10.* `scripts/build_domestic_presets.py` -> `data/domestic_seasons.json`, read by
   `engine/domestic.py` (`season("sma", "2019-20")`, `tournament_args(...)` for `play_tournament`, `match_total`).
   Seven seasons: **2015-16, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22** rebuilt from Cricsheet (groups as
   played; format checked against each season's knockout matches: Super League -> final in 2015-16, 2017-18 and
   2018-19, Super League -> semis in 2019-20, quarter-finals with the Plate winner in 2020-21, three preliminary
   quarter-finals in 2021-22; squads = every rated player who played that season, 11-22 per state, none short),
   and **2025-26** with the real groups and format (Super League -> final, Plate of 6 with its own final) and
   "latest" squads: each rated player's latest state if he played SMAT in 2022-23 or later or any T20 since July
   2023. Five states with no recent Cricsheet matches (Odisha, Maharashtra, Services, Jharkhand, Sikkim) are
   filled to 11 with their most recent other players, so they can include retired players (Saurabh Tiwary). The
   published 2025-26 squads are not used yet (name matching to Cricsheet ids is a follow-up). Venues: the season's
   group-stage and knockout grounds (no home grounds). Impact Player is off in every preset season. Each season
   plays end to end (`tests/test_domestic.py`); simulated first innings average 144-157.
5. **UI.** *Done 2026-11-10.* A **Mushtaq Ali Trophy** card inside Seasons & careers (not a fifth door, so
   home keeps four): season chips (2025-26 marked "latest squads"), a state to captain (Plate sides marked) or just
   watch, the groups and the real winner, the usual captaincy options. Server: run mode `domestic` (`/api/run`,
   `/api/meta` "domestic"). The 38 states have short codes and colours (`webui/teams.js`). Results: group tables,
   Super League tables, the knockout bracket (numbered ties of a round now share a column: "Quarter-finals",
   "Semi-finals"), the Plate's table and final in their own section, sides that went through marked in every
   table and the relegated side in red, and the champions card names who went up, who went down and the real
   winner. `tests/test_domestic.py` also runs a season through the server.
6. **State career** (separate step, after the trophy plays well).
7. **One-day cups** (separate step: the ODI league tier is its own piece of engine work).

Rough size: steps 1-5 are about the size of League Season plus one ratings rebuild; 6 is smaller than franchise
career (no auction); 7 is medium.

## 6. Risks

- **Thin ratings for small states.** Plate sides and some Elite sides (Sikkim, Arunachal, Manipur) have little data;
  their players shrink towards their state's level and that level towards the average state, so they look less
  weak than they are. Measured in step 2: Mizoram's first innings average 87 real v 115 simulated, Sikkim 96 v 109;
  the spread of state averages is 15 runs simulated v 22 real, and first-innings totals as a whole vary 18% too
  little. A weaker pull toward the average state (tested) helps SMAT predictions out of sample but costs a little
  elsewhere, so it was left at the tuned value. Options for later: a per-tier team shrinkage tuned on SMAT alone, or
  a rating uncertainty that the simulation draws from (thin players would then vary from match to match).
- **Recent seasons.** "This season" depends on name-matching published squads to Cricsheet ids, and on players
  whose last data is from 2021-22.
- **Shared IPL players.** Stars rarely play SMAT (they are on India duty), so a state's real-season squad is its
  domestic side, not its best possible XI. That's correct, but "Mumbai" won't have Rohit and Surya in most seasons.

## 7. Decisions needed

1. A fifth home door **Domestic cricket**, or a card inside **Seasons & careers**? *(A card, 2026-11-10.)*
2. Which seasons: only the complete ones (2018-19 to 2021-22), or also "This season" built from latest teams and
   published squads? *(Step 4 built both: six real seasons, and 2025-26 from latest teams; the published squads
   are not used yet.)*
3. First scope: SMAT only (steps 1-5), or SMAT plus the CSA T20 Challenge in the same rebuild? *(Step 2 did SMAT
   only; adding `ctc` later is one line and a rebuild.)*
4. State career and the one-day cups: later steps, or not at all?
